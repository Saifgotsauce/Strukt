Berghain is famous for banning cameras, which makes it strange how many photographers it has. Its head doorman is a working photographer with published books. Its upstairs bar has had photographs by one of the most celebrated photographers alive hanging on its walls since the night it opened. And for years, that same photographer was reportedly the only person allowed to take pictures inside.

The no-photo rule protects the people dancing. It has also had a side effect: the people who built the place are nearly as invisible as the crowd. Most coverage of Berghain is about getting past the door. This piece is about everyone behind it.

## The founders: Norbert Thormann and Michael Teufele

Berghain was [founded in 2004 by friends **Norbert Thormann** and **Michael Teufele**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghain), and its story starts a decade earlier. In the 1990s the pair ran a male-only fetish night called Snax, first held at the Bunker, a Second World War air-raid shelter that ravers had occupied and [run as a hardcore techno outpost until it was raided and closed in 1996](https://www.wikiwand.com/en/articles/Berghain).

From there they ran a gay sex club called Lab.oratory, and when they were offered more space next door, they opened a dance club in an old railway depot. That club was Ostgut. It ran from 1998 until January 2003, when the building was slated for demolition and later replaced by a large indoor arena. Deutsche Welle has written that Ostgut paved the way for Berghain, and that how the two founders got a gay crowd to dance to modern club music remains their secret. In 2004 they reopened in a 1950s power plant near Ostbahnhof, and Berghain began.

## The architects: Thomas Karsten and Alexandra Erhard

The building you queue outside is the work of Studio Karhard, the Berlin design firm of **[Thomas Karsten and Alexandra Erhard](https://www.berlintourism.org/berghain-berlin-guide/)**. They converted the old power plant into a club and, [according to Wikipedia's history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berghain), carried out its later interior and exterior expansions too.

Their approach was to strip the building back rather than decorate it: concrete, steel and enormous height left to speak for themselves. The result is a club that feels less like a designed interior than a found space, and that is exactly the effect that took design work to achieve.

## The art: Piotr Nathan, Wolfgang Tillmans and others

The first thing many people saw after the door, for thirteen years, was a wall by **Piotr Nathan**. His installation *Rituale des Verschwindens* (*Rituals of Disappearance*) was made of [175 aluminium panels](https://www.floornature.com/blog/berlin-famous-berghain-club-morphs-art-gallery-15746/) showing eruptions of elemental forces, and it [hung in the entrance hall from 2004 to 2017](https://groove.de/?p=187711). Nathan has described his work there as being about the way techno dissolves into dance and into a kind of shared ecstasy.

Upstairs, in Panorama Bar, **Wolfgang Tillmans** has had [three sets of work installed since the club opened](https://crackmagazine.net/article/long-reads/art-in-the-club-berghains-walls-welcome-transcendence/2), from abstract camera-less prints to large photographs of the human body. Some of those prints were also used as cover art for releases by Answer Code Request on Berghain's own label, Ostgut Ton. According to [Art Basel](https://www.artbasel.com/stories/wolfgang-tillmans-centre-pompidou-2025), Tillmans was the only person authorized to take photographs in Berghain, and hasn't used that permission since 2012.

They aren't the only artists in the building. Over the years, [**Joseph Marr**'s sculptures](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/chris-dercon-on-berghain-berlin-337755?amp=1) have been installed there, **Sarah Schönfeld** made a lamp, and **Carsten Nicolai** [made the power plant's heat visible](https://shop.legrandj.eu/products/berghain-kunst-im-klub-art-in-the-club). Even the doorman contributed: **Sven Marquardt** photographed his own colleagues for the club's walls.

## The door: Sven Marquardt

Marquardt is the one name from Berghain most people already know. He was [born in East Berlin in 1962](https://www.berlintourism.org/berghain-berlin-guide/), worked the door at Ostgut before Berghain existed, and is a photographer with published books, including an autobiography, *Die Nacht ist Leben*. He has said his decisions at the door are subjective, and [he has also said](https://www.playfulmag.com/post/the-evolution-of-berghain-from-ostgut-to-berlin-s-techno-mecca) that only a few of his team are allowed to choose who comes in.

That second point is the one that matters here. The door is a team, not one tattooed face. *We couldn't find the names of the rest of Berghain's door staff published anywhere, which is likely deliberate, and we respect that. But it means the people doing one of the hardest jobs in nightlife are known collectively, as "the bouncers," and never individually.*

## The sound: Tony Andrews and John Newsham

Berghain's main room runs on a Funktion-One system, the loudspeaker company founded in 1992 by [**Tony Andrews** and **John Newsham**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Funktion-One). Guides to the club describe the system as a [defining part of the room's design](https://www.nomadotravel.app/en/attractions/berghain) rather than an afterthought. (We wrote about how both men started out as touring crew in [our guide to becoming a live sound engineer](/journal/how-to-become-a-live-sound-engineer/).)

*But a system is only as good as the people who install and tune it, and we couldn't find who designed Berghain's installation, who tuned it for that concrete room, or who has looked after it since.*

## The names we still can't find

Here is what a club this famous still doesn't credit publicly, as far as we could find:

- *The lighting designers behind Berghain's main room and Panorama Bar.*
- *The sound engineers and system techs who keep the Funktion-One running across weekend-long parties.*
- *The bar, cloakroom, cleaning and security staff who make a party that runs from Saturday night to Monday morning possible.*
- *The rest of the door team.*

Some of that privacy is a choice the club and its staff are entitled to make. The photo ban exists so people can be themselves without being watched, and the same logic may well extend to its crew. But if you worked any of these jobs and would like your name on the record, [we'd love to hear from you](/call-for-creatives). We'll credit you however you want to be credited, including by first name only.

For thirteen years, everyone queuing for the cloakroom stood under Piotr Nathan's 175 panels. Most of them never knew his name.
