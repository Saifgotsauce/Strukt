Every April, someone famous turns up at the Do LaB stage at Coachella unannounced, and every April the clips that go round are of the crowd, the artist and the moment. Almost none of them show the structure everyone is dancing under, and almost nobody watching knows it was designed by a man who taught himself structural engineering, built by a crew led by his brothers, and dragged across California straight afterwards for another festival.

This is the Do LaB's year, told through the people who build it.

## The people

The Do LaB is a family business. Twin brothers **Josh** and **Jesse Flemming** and their younger brother **Dede** grew up in Pennsylvania, ran a DJ business there as teenagers, and moved to California in the late 1990s. NPR's account has Josh following Jesse west to [record audio for the ABC show *20/20*](https://www.npr.org/2022/04/22/1093356468/coachellas-music-fan-fave-do-lab-is-back), and Dede working as a production assistant on reality TV, before the three quit to throw parties full time. Their parents co-signed the lease on their first warehouse in downtown Los Angeles.

The roles have stayed remarkably clear. According to NPR, **Josh oversees the design and structure** of the stages, **Jesse curates the music**, and **Dede manages the logistics**. Their childhood friend **Jesse "Y2" Shannon** is [named as a co-founder](https://www.articulateshow.org/articulate/the-can-do-lab) too. And when Lightning in a Bottle, the festival that grew out of a birthday party for the twins, was held at Gold Creek Ranch in 2004, [Wikipedia lists **Dream Rockwell**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lightning_in_a_Bottle) as a co-founder and producer alongside Josh and Jesse.

Josh's skills are worth stopping on. In a 2018 interview with *Articulate*, the brothers said the architecture, structural engineering and set design behind their giant structures were all self-taught. "We were always building forts," Josh said.

## March: the warehouse

The season starts in Los Angeles. "From March until mid-June, we're in it every year," the brothers [told Pollstar in 2024](https://news.pollstar.com/2024/04/04/from-coachella-to-lightning-in-a-pottle-do-labs-busy-season-is-now/), and Jesse described the stretch as a lot of long days and long nights. The stages are designed and largely fabricated by the Do LaB themselves. As Dede [put it to Flaunt](https://www.flaunt.com/post/coachella-2023-do-lab), they design it, work with an engineer who's a close friend, do much of the fabrication themselves and build it all themselves.

*That engineer is never named in any interview we found.* One year's team, though, is on the record. For the 2013 stage, the architecture and design practice [Hunter Leggitt Studio](https://www.hunterleggitt.com/do-lab-stage-coachella-2013) was invited to collaborate on the engineering and construction, and its project page lists the credits in full: production design by the Do LaB and Vita Motus, engineering by MOR Engineers and Hunter Leggitt Studio, and fabrication and installation by the Do LaB and Hunter Leggitt Studio, with photos by Daniel Zetterstrom. *We couldn't find the individual engineers at MOR Engineers who worked on it, or the names of the Do LaB's own warehouse builders. If that's you, we want to credit you.*

## April: the field

In the last couple of weeks before doors, the crew moves to Indio to finish the build on site. [Spin's reporting](https://www.spin.com/?p=432466) describes each brother leading a team of workers who build the stage non-stop in the days before the gates open, then join the crowd to celebrate what they made.

The stage itself changes every few years, and each version has its own name and its own photographers, whom EDM Identity's [history of the stage](https://edmidentity.com/?p=228292) credits by name:

- **2010, "Misting Oasis"**, photographed by **Aaron Gautschi**
- **2014**, the year of "Nobody puts Do LaB in the corner!", photographed by **Daniel Zetterstrom**
- **2015 to 2016, "Big Fish"**, photographed by Daniel Zetterstrom
- **2017 to 2018, "Beacon"**, which the Do LaB designed with the studio [Vita Motus](https://vitamotus.com/?p=1682) as its most technically sophisticated stage up to that point
- **2019 to the present, "Warrior One"**, with later images credited to **DIVISUALS**, the photography name of [**Don Idio**](https://www.shambhalamusicfestival.com/blog/photo-albums-of-smf-2023-part-1), who also shoots Shambhala in British Columbia

*We couldn't confirm who at Vita Motus led the Beacon collaboration.* Other photographers credited for Do LaB images over the years include **Aaron Glassman** and **Gina Joy**, whose 2019 pictures [ran with a Grammy.com feature](https://grammy.com/news/elderbook-dance-spirit-kmln-justin-jay-discuss-magic-do-lab-coachella), and **WatcharaPhoto**, credited only by that name.

## April: the gates open

The Do LaB's first Coachella was small and a little unofficial. Accounts of the start date differ: NPR says the brothers have been there since 2004, when they snuck in some speakers after hours, while their own twentieth-anniversary look back with [The FADER](https://www.thefader.com/2025/04/11/history-of-do-lab-stage-coachella-20-anniversary-photos) begins in 2005, when Josh says they were given a chance to bring an art installation to the middle of the field. "We got a very tiny budget to come in and we set up a geodesic dome and an art piece," he told Pollstar.

Dede's memory of those early years is about care, not spectacle. They built a waterfall fountain beside the dome, he told NPR, and on 110-degree days they walked through the crowd handing out trays of sliced orange peels. They called it an oasis.

That's still the idea. The stage books its own artists and runs largely on its own within the wider festival, and Coachella's own art team values it. **Paul Clemente**, who manages Coachella's large-scale art program, told NPR: "It would be hard to imagine Coachella without the brothers involved."

## May: the second festival

The day Coachella ends, the season isn't half done. The brothers and their crew head roughly 240 miles to Buena Vista Lake for Lightning in a Bottle, their own festival, which Pollstar reported ran from May 22 to 27 in 2024. The Do LaB designs and builds that festival's stages and installations too, including the giant mushrooms that have also appeared at Coachella.

## June: the strike

By mid-June it's over, and the whole thing comes down again. Strike, the work of dismantling and packing a festival, is the least photographed part of any event, and the Do LaB's is no exception.

## The credits still missing

Two decades in, the Do LaB is one of the best-documented independent stages in the world, and it still has large gaps in its public credits:

- *The engineer who signs off on Josh's structures today.*
- *The builders, welders, riggers and fabricators on each team.*
- *The sound and lighting crews who run the stage over two Coachella weekends.*
- *The people who strike it every June.*

The brothers have always been generous about saying it's a team effort. We'd like to put that team's names on the record. If you've built a Do LaB stage, [tell us who you are and what you did](/call-for-creatives).
