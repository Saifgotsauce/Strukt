# AI tells and how to fix them

These patterns make copy sound generated or corporate. Scan every draft for them. Most are fine once in a while; the problem is reaching for them by reflex.

## Structures

**The contrast reflex.** "It's not X, it's Y." "This isn't just a magazine. It's a movement." "A scene isn't a lineup. It's…"
Fix: say the true thing directly. "The lineup is one night. The scene is everyone who keeps coming back after it."

**Fragment triplets.** "No budget. No connections. No problem." Three short fragments in a row, used for drama.
Fix: one full sentence that carries the same idea, or a single fragment at most.

**Split hooks.** Two short sentences where one would read naturally: "You probably know who was playing. You probably don't know who made that moment."
Fix: join them so the thought flows. "You'll always remember who was playing, but almost nobody knows who built the moment that made the whole room lose it."

**The reader quiz opener.** "Think about the last time…", "Picture this:", "Imagine a world where…", "Have you ever wondered…"
Fix: open inside a real moment. Put the reader there instead of asking them to imagine it.

**Ending on a question.** "So what will you build?" "Are you ready?"
Fix: end on an image, a fact or a quote.

**Explaining the metaphor.** "Our logo is made of dots, just like a scene is made of people."
Fix: trust the image. Use it, then move on, or let the reader make the connection.

**Colon reveals everywhere.** "Here's the thing: …" "The result: …"
Fix: allow one per piece at most.

**Anaphora on autopilot.** "Somebody did this. Somebody did that. Somebody did the other." Repetition can work once in a piece when it builds to something; used as a default, it reads as a template.

**Neat summary paragraphs.** "In short…", "Ultimately…", "At the end of the day…"
Fix: cut them. The ending should be the strongest line, not a recap.

## Words and phrases

Cut or replace on sight:
- delve, dive into, navigate, landscape, realm, tapestry, testament, embark, journey
- unlock, empower, elevate, seamless, robust, leverage, curated (unless something was literally curated), game-changer, cutting-edge
- vibrant, bustling, electric (for crowds), unforgettable, magical, iconic (unless it truly is)
- "in today's world", "now more than ever", "whether you're X or Y", "from X to Y" used as a fake range
- "a sea of" anything
- "it's worth noting", "importantly", "notably"

## Punctuation

- Em dashes: one per piece at most. Use commas, full stops or brackets instead.
- Exclamation marks: none in editorial copy. One in a caption if someone genuinely earned it.
- Rhetorical questions: none in a row, and never as a headline unless it's a real question the piece answers.

## Truth checks

- Would someone who was in the room recognise this detail? (Nobody stops mid-sentence on a dance floor.)
- Is every claim either common knowledge, from the interview, or sourced?
- Did a "quote" actually get said by a real person? If not, it's a gap: `[quote from name, role]`.
