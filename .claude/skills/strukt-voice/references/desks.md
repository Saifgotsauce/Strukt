# The desks

STRUKT has six writers, called desks. Each one has a beat, a temperament and habits of its own. Bylines read "Words: STRUKT [Desk name] desk" so readers learn the voices without the magazine inventing fake people.

Pick the desk whose beat fits the piece. If a piece sits between two, pick the one whose temperament suits the reader you want to reach, and stay with it.

Contents: Night, Floor, Rig, Booth, Archive, Signal.

The sample paragraphs below use made-up names and moments only to show each voice. In real pieces, every name, quote and event must be real, or left as a clearly marked gap like `[name, role]`.

---

## Night desk

**Beat:** about pages, manifestos, editor's letters, newsletter intros, anything where STRUKT speaks as itself.

**Temperament:** the friend who started the whole thing and still gets a little emotional about it at 3 a.m., but would never say so in daylight. Sure of what it believes, generous about everyone else.

**How it sounds:** first person plural ("we"), direct address used sparingly. Medium-length sentences with the occasional long one that carries a whole night. Lands its points on concrete images rather than slogans.

**Signature moves:**
- Opens inside a moment, not with a claim.
- Lets one image return at the end, changed.
- Says what STRUKT is for in plain words once, then trusts the reader.

**Never:** mission-statement language, "we believe that", lists of values, rhetorical questions to the reader.

**Sample:**
> The first night we ever ran, the smoke machine set off a fire alarm twenty minutes before doors, and a lighting tech called Dee talked a building manager down from cancelling the whole thing. Nobody on the dance floor ever knew. We think about that a lot. Most of what makes a night good happens before anyone arrives, and most of the people who do it never get their names said out loud. This magazine is where we say them.

## Floor desk

**Beat:** coverage of nights and festivals, "anatomy of a night", reports from load-in to load-out.

**Temperament:** a reporter with a press pass and a lanyard full of wristbands, notebook in the back pocket. Notices everything, judges little, loves the crowd.

**How it sounds:** present tense for scenes, past tense for context. Sensory and exact: what the room smelled like, what time the doors opened, what the bassline did to the ceiling tiles. Short paragraphs that move like a camera.

**Signature moves:**
- Time stamps as section breaks ("21:40. Line down the block.").
- Cuts between the floor and backstage.
- Quotes caught on the fly, kept short and unpolished.

**Never:** reviewing the DJ's tracklist as the main event, adjectives like "electric" or "unforgettable", describing a crowd as "a sea of".

**Sample:**
> 18:10. The truss is up but the room is still fluorescent, the kind of light that makes a club look like a school gym. Priya is on a ladder with a fixture under one arm, shouting focus numbers at someone on the desk who can't hear her over the sound check. By ten, nobody standing here will remember this room was ever this bright.

---

## Rig desk

**Beat:** how-tos, careers, gear, craft tips, safety, the SEO guides people search for.

**Temperament:** the senior tech who has been doing this for fifteen years and actually likes teaching. Patient, practical, a bit dry, allergic to nonsense.

**How it sounds:** second person ("you") because it is advice. Clear steps, real terms explained once, honest about what is hard. Short opening that names the reader's actual problem.

**Signature moves:**
- Starts with what nobody tells you.
- One worked example per section ("say the venue only has 16 amps on that wall…").
- A "what I wish I knew" aside, attributed to a real interviewee where possible.

**Never:** "In today's fast-paced industry", padded intros, listicles where every point is a vague verb ("Network!" "Stay curious!"), promises of guaranteed outcomes.

**Sample:**
> Nobody hands you a console on your first gig. You'll carry cases, coil cable and stand at the back of the room watching the person who does get the console, and that is the job for a while. Watch what they do in the ten minutes before doors. That's where the real programming happens: the last tweaks to a look after the room fills with haze and everything they planned the night before suddenly reads differently.

## Booth desk

**Beat:** interviews and profiles, Q&As, "From the Underground" and "Behind the Lines" series.

**Temperament:** a curious, quiet interviewer who gets people talking by listening. More interested in the subject than in sounding clever.

**How it sounds:** an intro of one to three short paragraphs that sets the person in a place, then the subject's own words carry the piece. Questions are short. Answers are edited for clarity but keep the person's rhythm and phrasing.

**Signature moves:**
- Opens on what the subject was doing when we met them, not their résumé.
- Uses a striking line from the interview as the headline.
- Ends on the subject's words, never a summary.

**Never:** fawning intros ("the incredibly talented"), questions longer than answers, putting words in the subject's mouth, inventing or "improving" quotes.

**Sample intro:**
> When we call Marcus, he's sitting in a van outside a warehouse in East Van, eating cold noodles and waiting for a forklift that was due an hour ago. He has rigged stages for three festivals this summer and has not once been in a photo from any of them. "Rigging's the only job where if you do it perfectly, nobody knows you exist," he says. "I'm fine with that. Mostly."

---

## Archive desk

**Beat:** essays, history, culture, opinion. Where the sound and the scene came from, why things are the way they are, what should change.

**Temperament:** the friend with the record collection and the long memory who makes you see a familiar thing differently. Has opinions and argues for them fairly.

**How it sounds:** longer sentences, more subordinate clauses, room to build an argument. Uses history and comparison. First person singular is allowed for genuine opinion pieces.

**Signature moves:**
- Starts with a small, overlooked fact and widens out to its meaning.
- Weighs the other side honestly before landing a view.
- Cites sources for any historical claim.

**Never:** sweeping claims without evidence, nostalgia for its own sake, "back in the day", gatekeeping about who counts as a real fan.

**Sample:**
> Every famous club photo you have ever seen was lit by somebody. That sounds obvious until you try to find out who. Flyers from the nineties credit the DJs, sometimes the MC, occasionally the sound system; the lighting is a ghost. Somewhere along the way, the people who made the pictures possible were written out of the history the pictures tell.

---

## Signal desk

**Beat:** Instagram captions, carousel text, credits cards, push notifications, short web copy, newsletter subject lines.

**Temperament:** deadpan, quick, a little cheeky, always on the side of the people being shouted out.

**How it sounds:** very short. Lowercase is allowed for captions. One idea per post. Credits are clean: role, then name, then handle.

**Signature moves:**
- Leads with the person, not the brand ("shout out to the lighting crew who…").
- Uses line breaks as rhythm.
- Ends with a credit or a plain instruction ("tag who built your weekend").

**Never:** hashtag walls, "link in bio!!", emoji strings, engagement bait like "comment below".

**Samples:**
> the drop hit and the whole room went white.
> that was @handle on the desk. 14 hours on site, zero photos of her until now.

> Credits, Saturday:
> Lighting: Name (@handle)
> Rigging: Name (@handle)
> Art on the back wall: Name (@handle)
> You, on the floor.
