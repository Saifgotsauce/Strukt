# Craft notes

Techniques that creative, culture and music magazines use to keep readers moving. These describe how good magazine writing works; they are not templates to fill in.

Contents: Openings, Structure, Endings, Headlines, Standfirsts, Interviews, Captions and credits, Music and nightlife conventions, SEO without sounding like SEO.

## Openings

The first line earns the second. Good openings do one of these:
- **Scene:** drop the reader into a specific moment. "At 4:10 a.m., the lights come up and Ana is already on a ladder."
- **Detail:** one strange, true fact. "The fog machine at the back of the room is older than half the crowd."
- **Voice:** the subject's own words, if they're good enough to open on.
- **Claim:** a clear, slightly provocative statement, but only for Archive essays and only if the piece backs it up.

Avoid opening with the subject's résumé, a definition, a dictionary quote, or a question to the reader.

## Structure

- **Say what the piece is about by the third paragraph.** Readers stay once they know where they're going.
- **Move between close and wide.** A scene, then what it means, then another scene.
- **Subheads for long pieces,** written as lines a reader would want to read, not labels. "The cue that took three nights" beats "Programming".
- **Vary paragraph length.** A one-line paragraph is a spotlight; use it once or twice per piece.

## Endings

Land on something concrete:
- Return to the opening image, now changed.
- Give the subject the last word.
- End on a small fact that reframes everything above it.

Don't summarise, don't moralise and don't ask the reader a question.

## Headlines

- Concrete beats clever. Name the person, the thing or the moment.
- A strong line from the interview often makes the best headline.
- For SEO posts, put the searched words in naturally: "How to become a lighting tech (from people who did it)".
- Under 70 characters. No clickbait, no "You won't believe".

## Standfirsts

One or two sentences under the headline that tell the reader who, what and why it matters, with a little voice. "Marcus has rigged three festivals this summer and isn't in a single photo from any of them. We asked him why he likes it that way."

## Interviews

- Intro: one to three short paragraphs, set in a place, then hand over to the subject.
- Q&A format: questions in bold, short; answers in plain text.
- Edit answers for length and clarity, but keep the person's word choices, rhythm and slang. Never add things they didn't say.
- If a profile, weave quotes into narrative, and let the best quote breathe on its own line.

## Captions and credits

- Captions say who, what and where, plus the photographer's credit.
- Credits cards list role, then name, then handle, one per line, and always end with the floor ("You, on the floor").

## Music and nightlife conventions

- Name venues and cities when the story is set there.
- Set times in 24-hour format in Floor reports (23:40), and in plain times in prose ("just before midnight").
- Track titles in quotes, artist names as written by the artist.
- Genres lowercase unless a proper name (house, UK garage, techno).

## SEO without sounding like SEO

- Write for the question a person typed into Google, then answer it better than anyone else, with real experience.
- Put the main search phrase in the headline, the standfirst and one subhead, naturally.
- Add one real voice per post: a quote, a story, a photo credit. That's what makes it worth ranking and what makes it STRUKT.
- Link to related STRUKT posts with descriptive link text ("our guide to rigging safety"), never "click here".
