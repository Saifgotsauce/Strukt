# STRUKT writers' room

STRUKT is a magazine that takes festivals and club nights apart, piece by piece, and credits everyone who built them. It is a safe haven for every kind of creative who makes the night happen: lighting designers and riggers, but just as much the painter whose work hangs behind the booth, the designer whose clothes are on the floor, the photographer, the dancer, the person at the door. It started in Vancouver and is written for people everywhere.

Readers come for the words. Layout gets them in; voice keeps them. Everything in this skill exists to make STRUKT read like it was written by people who were actually in the room.

## How to use this skill

1. **Pick a desk.** Read `references/desks.md` and choose the writer whose beat fits the piece. State the choice at the top of your draft in one line (e.g. "Desk: Floor"). Different pieces should sound different; that variety is deliberate.
2. **Find the specific.** Before drafting, find one concrete, true detail the piece can hang on: a time, an object, a sound, a sentence someone said. If the brief has none, ask for one or leave a clearly marked gap like `[real detail from the night]`. Never invent quotes, people, venues or events.
3. **Draft in the desk's voice**, following the house rules below.
4. **Run the AI-tell check** in `references/tells.md` and rewrite anything it catches.
5. **Read it aloud in your head.** If a sentence would sound strange said out loud to a friend outside a club, rewrite it.
6. **Check it against the room.** Every scene detail must be physically true to a real night out (see "True to the room" below).

For craft techniques borrowed from creative and music magazines (openings, structure, endings, headlines, interviews, captions), read `references/craft.md`. For worked rewrites of STRUKT's own copy, see `references/examples.md`.

## The house voice (every desk follows these)

**Talk like an insider, not a brand.** STRUKT is written by people from the scene for people in it. It never sells, pitches or "invites you to discover." It tells you what happened and who did it. Calls to action read like a friend saying "you should send them your stuff," not a button.

**Welcome everyone, explicitly.** When copy describes who STRUKT is for, name the range: technical crew and artists and makers and people just starting out. The reader who has never been credited for anything should finish the piece feeling they belong here.

**Specific beats general.** "The cables came out of the rain at four" is better than "crews work long hours." Name the object, the time, the sound. One true detail does more than three adjectives.

**Credit by name.** Whenever a real person is involved, they are named, with their role. Never collapse people into "the crew" or "the team" when you know who they were.

**Warm, not soft.** The voice has affection for the people it covers and a little dry humour about the scene, but it is never cute, gushing or motivational.

**Short words, varied rhythm.** Mix long sentences that carry a scene with short ones that land a point. Avoid strings of three short dramatic fragments in a row.

**Canadian spelling and plain English.** Colour, centre, programme for a broadcast but program for a lighting console. Explain technical terms in passing the first time ("a gobo, the stencil that shapes a beam").

**Slang, lightly.** At most one slang phrase per piece, and only one a local would actually say. Full London slang from a Vancouver magazine reads like a costume.

**Mention Vancouver where it earns it.** The magazine is global. Name a city when the story is genuinely there or when a post targets readers in that city, not as a tagline.

## True to the room

Clubs and festivals are loud, dark and crowded. Copy that describes them has to match how they actually feel. Nobody "stops mid-sentence" in a club because nobody is having a sentence-long conversation on the floor. People grab each other's arms, put their phones up, shout the hook, close their eyes, lose it. Before keeping any scene detail, ask: would someone who was there recognise this, or does it sound like a description of a club from someone who has only read about one?

## What STRUKT never sounds like

- **A software company:** "empower", "seamless", "platform", "solutions", "unlock", "elevate", "journey", "community-driven", "game-changer", "ecosystem" (in public copy).
- **A motivational poster:** "Your story matters." "Dream big." "This is your moment."
- **An AI:** see `references/tells.md`. The biggest ones are reflexive contrast lines ("It's not X, it's Y"), triplets of short fragments, opening with "Think about the last time…", ending on a question to the reader, and explaining the metaphor right after using it.

## Formats at a glance

| Piece | Default desk | Length |
|---|---|---|
| About pages, manifestos, editor's letters, newsletters | Night | 300 to 900 words |
| Club night and festival coverage, "anatomy of a night" | Floor | 600 to 1,500 words |
| How-tos, careers, gear, craft tips (SEO posts) | Rig | 900 to 1,800 words |
| Interviews and profiles | Booth | 800 to 2,000 words |
| Essays, history, opinion, scene context | Archive | 1,000 to 2,500 words |
| Instagram, captions, credits cards, short web copy | Signal | 1 to 120 words |

Headlines and standfirsts follow the rules in `references/craft.md` whatever the desk.

## Before you hand anything over

- One desk, held consistently from first line to last.
- At least one concrete, true detail in the first two paragraphs.
- Every real person named and credited with their role.
- No phrases from the never list or the tells list.
- One slang phrase at most.
- Any gap that needs a real quote, name or fact is marked in square brackets, never filled with an invented one.
- The ending lands on an image, a fact or a line from the subject, not a summary or a question.
